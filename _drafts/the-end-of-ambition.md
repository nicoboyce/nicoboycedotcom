There's a new Star Wars film this weekend and we went to the Imax in London to watch it. I figured the Mandalorian and Grogu film wasn't going to be groundbreaking but would definitely be exciting on a huge screen. This instinct was correct. I've seen all the Star Wars films plenty of times and most of the recent television stuff and I can confirm that the Mandalorian film is basically fine.

It's the same deal with the new Forza Horizon game. Friends may remember that Rosie and I did a Forza Horizon podcast through 2020. We went on a tour of locations from FH4 and appeared on Canadian radio. I had a few world records in FH5 for a while and did a couple of collabs with Chevrolet and the Petersen museum. We also did streams and tutorials for GFinity. I'm not really a video games person but I've played all of this series, probably to an extent many would consider excessive.

The new game was released this week, Forza Horizon 6. It is set in Japan, which many were looking forward to. It's perfectly okay.

It looks incredible. The lighting, especially reflections and shadows, are such a huge step forward from previous games. Areas like the Daikoku car park are replicated in stunning detail. It is an improvement over the previous version in all the ways we all expect from a video game sequel. It's not fun though.

Lots of things are impressive but I don't want them. On paper a Tesla is an impressive piece of kit but I don't like being in one.

As you know I watch a lot of motorsport. Single seaters from F1 and Indycar down to F4 level. Nascar, touring cars, endurance, rallycross. You know what literally none of these series have? A sudden little pop of a crest into a hidden dip into a mid radius 90 degree banked sweeper type corner.

They don't have this because everyone would crash into the outside wall every time and it would be horrible. You can't judge your braking at all, your wheels are off the ground when you need to be stopping, you can't lift for the crest because you're pushed into a spin by the non player cars. Every race in this game has exactly that a bunch of times. You find yourself pressing Y to rewind over and over. You're pitched into a spin or you nose into the wall a dozen times a race (and I'm not turning off the rewind while I have other things to be doing thank you!) and so every victory feels hollow because it is.

The rest of the game? Well, it does the job. There are some story elements and you can earn cash to buy more cars and cosmetic items for your garage if you want. It's not exciting or interesting, it's just easy to sell. Did you like the last game? It's that, with better graphics and a bigger map, so just buy this.

What's different with treasure cars to barn finds? Why does the game throw cars at you in the first few hours? It feels so unrewarding.

I don't want to be forced to build user generated content to access things I want to do in the game but I can see why the studio decided that was a good idea. Get the players to build tracks and stuff, saves them a job. Maybe that is what will save the game, if actually good races emerge.

Grogu and Forza Horizon 6 are symptoms of the same modern problem. It's not that either are bad, it's just that there's no ambition to be exceptional or even particularly good. They are within the window of acceptable outcomes.

This is the defining feature of our time just now. It's not that they are good or not good — quality itself is no longer relevant. They are going to perform exactly as planned. They will make a good return on the investment but there won't be any big runaway success, no unexpected supply chain issues with merch, no need to rush the follow-up. Just planned mediocrity — earning more cash from the audience they have already built up and doing absolutely nothing surprising or innovative. They do a job and people who have played the previous games or watched the previous films will spend the cash and not be too excited and not be too disappointed. That's the beginning and the end of the ambition.

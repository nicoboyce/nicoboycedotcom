It's impressive to me when someone knows it's time to draw the line. Imagine saying "enough is enough" but doing that before you realise "okay it was enough some way back and now it's just far too late" because it's something I can only imagine.

Perhaps I have a reputation for grouchiness. I definitely have no tolerance for bullshit. But it takes me far too long to give up on someone, however they behave.

Maybe part of it is that I know I wasn't always perfect. Without a doubt nowadays I'm dangerously near ideal. In the dark and distant past some might refer to as the 2000s, I was pretty difficult to know. Is that why I can be too forgiving?

The reverse happens to others, from my perspective. There are people I thought would never lose but in hindsight we were on very different trajectories when we intersected. Most of the problems in my twenties came from a crushing need to be involved, to be inside the thing rather than observing. Now I'm not trying to join in but I'm also not interested in watching. It's something else.

*

A colleague mentioned they had been reading this site. I have a lot of projects on the go, as you can see. How do I have the time? How do I decide what's next? Honestly I just don't worry about it that much. Today I had a little time and I got to the bike track. Before work I had a little keyboard jam, working on some ideas. I'm doing what I'm doing and people in my life are welcome to join in or do something else or maybe I want in on what you're up to. There's no pressure.

If you do push hard on others then the instinct is to get out of that situation. It's like pushing down with your thumb on a marble on a table. It'll shoot off in some other direction. I've had plenty of friends do that over the years. That's okay!

*

There came a point a few years ago where I had to make decisions about my extended family. These people were always treating each other horribly. Any conversation was always "Don't tell them I said that" and it was just this eternal pointless weaving of nonsense. The worst culprits were my mother and sister, who thought they were talented at lying and manipulating but they should definitely never play poker.

This was starting to affect my child and I realised there was good reason to stop indulging their petty games. My responsibilities had changed and ultimately it wasn't a difficult decision to cut contact. It's not something I have given a lot of thought to since, because it was so obviously correct and appropriate.
